## 0. Truth law

The owner-supplied ten-automation screenshot remains `CREATOR_CLAIM`. Its advertised prices are not buyer evidence, not market clearing prices, and not UberBond revenue.

Evidence classes used below:

- `OFFICIAL_PRODUCT_FACT`: a current official product, pricing, support, regulator, or developer page.
- `SELLER_SURVEY`: research published by a vendor or industry platform; useful but not independent market truth.
- `VENDOR_CLAIM`: a seller's own performance, customer-count, case-study, or benefit statement.
- `REPOSITORY_FACT`: current UberBond source, tests, receipts, or current-state canon.
- `INFERENCE`: UberBond's synthesis from the cited evidence.
- `EXTERNAL_PROOF_REQUIRED`: cannot become true from repository work alone.

A feature being sold by established software proves an operating market mechanism. It does **not** prove that UberBond can charge the screenshot price, acquire a buyer, deliver profitably, or retain the buyer.

# 1. Executive decision

The ten offers are commercially real mechanisms, but the screenshot's packaging is economically misleading. Most are already inexpensive features inside mature platforms. The correct UberBond move is therefore **not** ten standalone mini-SaaS clones.

The strongest current wedge is:

> **Home-Service Revenue Leak Recovery + Evidence**, initially for HVAC / plumbing / electrical operators and, preferably, the agencies/MSPs that serve them.

The wedge composes existing UberBond primitives around systems the buyer already uses:

`lead/call event → eligibility/consent → fast response → appointment/quote state → estimate follow-up → review/retention trigger → payment/delivery/acceptance evidence → dashboard/incident history`

The winning differentiation is **cross-system recovery, evidence, reconciliation, and accountable outcome lineage**, not merely the ability to send a text or create a CRM row.

This explicitly challenges the earlier provisional “missed-call text-back + estimate follow-up” pair. Both mechanisms are real, but both are already native features in mainstream platforms. They remain useful modules; they are too commoditized to be the whole offer.

## 5.3 Kill conditions

- **Outbound:** pause on sender-health degradation, rising hard bounces, complaints, provenance failures, provider-policy uncertainty, or no qualified conversations after a predeclared bounded sample.
- **Partners:** kill an archetype when implementation/support burden destroys wholesale margin, the partner does not own trusted downstream access, or channel conflict exceeds incremental distribution value.
- **Product-led:** kill assets that produce no qualified usage or evidence of a downstream problem after a bounded observation window.
- **Owned content:** kill topics with no path to a proof tool, buyer question, or product-family thesis.
- **Agent ecosystems:** kill surfaces that merely duplicate a website/dashboard and do not create lower-friction verified utility.
- **Paid media:** do not scale before a measured accepted-delivery funnel demonstrates positive contribution after media, sales, support, refund and provider costs.
- **Marketplaces:** kill any marketplace dependency that forces unsupported claims, destructive discounting, exclusive lock-in or unsafe data sharing.

## 5.4 Proposed diversification policy

`PROPOSED_POLICY`, not observed truth:

- direct outbound and partner distribution are complementary, not substitutes;

## 6.2 Current provider/payment facts

- **Stripe:** Egypt is not in the current standard-account country list on Stripe's global availability page. Do not design the primary Egypt-founder route around a direct Stripe account.
- **Lemon Squeezy:** Egypt is listed for bank payouts. Lemon Squeezy states that it acts as Merchant of Record for its supported ecommerce transactions. Standard ecommerce pricing is 5% + $0.50, with documented adders for some international, PayPal and subscription transactions. Net sales are held for 13 days and payouts are created twice monthly; payout fees may apply. Merchant identity verification may be required.
- **Paddle:** says it works with software businesses worldwide except its explicit unsupported list; Egypt is not on that unsupported list. Paddle is a Merchant of Record for supported transactions. Product eligibility and supplier verification still require actual approval.
- **Egyptian Tax Authority:** the ETA operates electronic-invoice/e-receipt systems and states that taxpayers were phased into electronic invoices for services/goods. Its current exported-services guidance says qualifying exported services can be zero-rated and describes documentary requirements. The exact treatment for UberBond depends on legal form, registration, activity, customer/location, service classification, payment documentation and the actual provider/MoR contract.

Therefore the software can preserve the evidence, but the founder's exact registration/tax/KYC route remains `EXTERNAL_PROOF_REQUIRED` and should be checked with an Egyptian tax/legal professional before live commercial activation. This document is not legal or tax advice.
