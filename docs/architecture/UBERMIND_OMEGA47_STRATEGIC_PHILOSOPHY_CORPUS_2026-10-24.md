## Part 1: Philosophical and strategic foundation

### 1. Realpolitik and power mechanics
1. **Niccolò Machiavelli** — *The Prince*; *Discourses on Livy*. Founder thesis: practical power, preserving productive assets, pragmatic strategic calculation, results and stable institutions rather than sentimental assumptions. "Ends justify means" is a simplified interpretation, not a license or automatic literal quotation. Applied lens: stakeholder/veto maps, reputational costs, coalitions, counterparty alternatives, legitimacy, dependence and long-horizon retaliation. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1232 ; *Discourses* requires verification of edition/translation rights before copying.
2. **Robert Greene** — *The 48 Laws of Power*; *The 33 Strategies of War*. Founder thesis: human motivations, tactical information discipline, self-presentation, reputation, positional leverage and awareness of adversarial incentives. Applied lens: asymmetric negotiations, information boundaries, switching costs, reputation, conflict de-escalation and defensive detection of manipulation. Modern copyrighted texts: retrieve licensed editions or bibliographic notes only. Don't implement fraud, concealed material terms or fake social proof.
3. **Otto von Bismarck** — collected speeches and memoirs. Founder thesis: Realpolitik, coalition construction, material constraints, institutional bargaining, timing and realistic compromise. Applied lens: interests of distinct stakeholders, leverage from credible alternatives, strategic sequencing and preventing self-defeating conflicts. Exact edition and historical quotation require source checking.

### 2. Rational self-interest and pragmatism
4. **Ayn Rand** — *Atlas Shrugged*; *The Virtue of Selfishness*. Founder thesis: productive achievement, voluntary exchange, entrepreneurial independence and rational self-interest over externally imposed guilt. Applied lens: opportunity cost, legitimate value creation, founder autonomy, contractual consent, productive competence and future option value. Her philosophical conclusions are debated, not universally valid facts. Copyrighted: do not copy whole books.
5. **Max Stirner** — *The Ego and Its Own* (also translated *The Unique and Its Property*). Founder thesis: radical individual agency, interrogation of nonbinding conventions, independent goals and pragmatic refusal of empty status games. Applied lens: distinguish legal commitments and real dependencies from traditions, fear of disapproval and nonbinding expectations. Preserve agreed obligations and other people's autonomy.

### 3. Strategic warfare and game theory
6. **Sun Tzu** — *The Art of War*. Founder thesis: asymmetric positioning, information advantage, defeating costly conflict before it begins, conserving energy and surprising competitors. Applied lens: differentiated niches, concentration of resources, strategic alliances, logistics, not attacking unwinnable markets, credible preemption. Text: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17405 (Lionel Giles translation; check the applicable location's rights before reproduction). Wartime deception is NOT permission to deceive customers about material deal facts.
7. **John von Neumann** (with Oskar Morgenstern) — *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior*. Founder thesis: game-theoretic incentives, strategic moves, payout structures and competitive games including zero-sum. Applied lens: payoff matrices, credible commitment, signaling, multi-party bargaining, option value and equilibrium scenarios. **Not every business interaction is zero-sum**; model positive-sum trades, repeated games, imperfect information and mechanism design as appropriate.

### 4. Separation of law and morality; legal realism
8. **H. L. A. Hart** — *The Concept of Law*. Founder thesis: distinguish law's institutional validity from disputed moral intuitions; understand enforceable rules, recognition, adjudication and interpretations. Applied lens: find actual binding jurisdictional requirements and legal grey areas with counsel, not infer that anything legal is wise, fair, safe or risk-free. The simple claim that law and morality are absolutely separate is a contested oversimplification. Scholarly context: https://academic.oup.com/stanford-scholarship-online/book/22700/chapter-abstract/183097985 .
9. **Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr.** — *The Common Law*; *The Path of the Law*. Founder thesis: forecast how courts, enforcement authorities and counterparties respond in practice. Applied lens: expected liability, enforcement uncertainty, litigation cost, contract remedies, facts a court would care about, not mere nominal statutory text. His "bad man" heuristic is descriptive, not a directive to misbehave. Context: https://academic.oup.com/book/50308/chapter-abstract/422787979 .
