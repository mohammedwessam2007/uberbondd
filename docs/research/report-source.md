## Executive result

UberBond should prepare exactly one commercial canary:

> **White-label Lead-Path Revenue Leak Evidence Sprint** for agencies serving HVAC, plumbing, or electrical companies.

It should retain exactly one fallback:

> **AI Agent Acceptance Evidence Sprint** for AI implementation agencies or software teams that can provide a safe authorized sandbox.

Neither is a live experiment. UberBond still has zero customers, zero cleared revenue, zero accepted deliveries, and zero retained customers. The canary price of `$450` and fallback price of `$900` are explicit hypotheses, not willingness-to-pay evidence.

Partner/white-label is the primary distribution architecture. A safe product-led evidence asset and owned evidence content support it. Governed direct outbound is the third motion, but it remains blocked by legal, provider, domain, mailbox, sender-health, cost, budget, and authority gates.

The strongest negative conclusion is equally important: the obvious automations are already cheap, bundled features. UberBond must not build or sell a generic CRM, dashboard, sequencer, AI-worker swarm, or bundle of commodity automations. Its potential differentiator is independent evidence, cross-system reconciliation, recovery, bounded delivery, external acceptance, and economic lineage.

## Finding 1 — the first buyer/problem pair

Jobber's 2026 seller survey says more than 55% of surveyed customers expected a response within an hour and highlights quote and communication burden. Its HVAC section reports high AI adoption but slow lead response among surveyed/platform cohorts. [Jobber 2026 Home Service Trends](https://www.getjobber.com/home-service-trends-report/).

Housecall Pro's separate 2026 seller survey of more than 1,100 US homeowners reports repair-led demand, with HVAC and plumbing among leading categories, and names sudden problems, worsening issues, and routine maintenance as predictable triggers. It also reports that 72% of respondents would pay more to resolve an emergency within 24 hours. The source discloses an online non-probability survey and says results should be interpreted directionally. [Housecall Pro 2026 report](https://www.housecallpro.com/resources/home-service-spending-report/).

These sources support a current response/estimate/recovery problem mechanism. They do not prove that UberBond can acquire an agency, that the agency will pay `$450`, that a finding causes recovered revenue, or that the work will renew.

The selected buyer is an agency rather than a single home-service operator because one relationship can expose several downstream accounts and permit white-label delivery. That is an allocation inference, not observed UberBond partner demand.

## Finding 5 — exact canary and fallback

### Canary

One downstream site, one primary public lead path, public-surface observation, and up to five agency-authorized synthetic scenarios. The output is a partner-brandable evidence pack with reproduction steps, evidence references, severity, limitations, and a remediation backlog.

It excludes real lead/customer contact, unauthorized forms, calls, SMS/email, production writes, private customer data, remediation, and revenue guarantees. Delivery begins only after cleared payment and complete authorized inputs. The SLA hypothesis is five business days with a 180-founder-minute ceiling.

### Fallback

One versioned agent, up to 25 buyer-approved scenarios, repeated runs when nondeterminism matters, final-state comparison, procedure/tool checks, unauthorized-extra-action checks, failure clustering, and a versioned acceptance report.

It excludes production traffic, real customer data, live financial/account/identity actions, penetration testing, remediation, and self-certified customer acceptance. The detailed contracts are in [offer-factory-research-packet.json](../../artifacts/offer-factory-research-packet.json).

The repository's previous `$15/month` two-mailbox ceiling conflicts with the current public Maildoso minimum. The custom exact-quantity price is not public. Procurement is therefore blocked pending an exact quote or a different verified provider route.

Capacity math:

- current: zero authorized messages;
- proposed smallest canary: 2 mailboxes × 10/day = 20/day, cost unknown;
- Maildoso public minimum: 30 × 15/day = 450/day, known mailbox cost `$75/month`, overprovisioned;
- 1,500/day: 100 mailboxes at the provider cap, exact cost unknown, rejected before evidence.

The economic equation is:

`cleared payments - refunds - disputes - payment fees - acquisition infrastructure - data/verification - model/browser - delivery provider costs - support labor - founder-minute cost`

With zero sales, contribution is negative by every real cost. With one hypothetical `$450` cleared sale, contribution remains unknown until all cost terms and founder minutes are receipted. No conversion or expected-revenue number is manufactured. See [outreach-capacity-economics.json](../../artifacts/outreach-capacity-economics.json).

## Finding 7 — lawful and provider-safe outbound is jurisdiction-specific

The US [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) requires accurate identity/subject information, a valid address, and opt-out handling. UK [ICO B2B guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/) distinguishes corporate subscribers from sole traders and partnerships while retaining identity, opt-out, and personal-data duties.

Canada is disabled without qualified consent evidence under [official CASL guidance](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email). Australia is likewise disabled without qualified consent and compliant unsubscribe handling under [ACMA guidance](https://www.acma.gov.au/avoid-sending-spam). Other routes require exact local adjudication; the report does not invent a global legal conclusion.

Google and Yahoo both publish authentication and complaint requirements; Google says bulk senders need SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and one-click unsubscribe, and the complaint rate should remain below 0.3%. UberBond's existing complaint circuit breaker is stricter at 0.1%. [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) and [Yahoo sender practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/).
