05. BASELINE ARCHITECTURE: WHAT THE ENGINE ACTUALLY WOULD BE
================================================================================

[DESIGN] CHIMERA has one fast shared neural chess representation, conditioned
by compact style/plan states, plus optional bounded numerical chess search.

A. Input:
   - Legal board position including side to move, castling rights, en passant,
     clocks and relevant game history.
   - Persistent strategic objective/plan representation (if any).
   - Rule-of-chess enforcement, no hallucinated illegal moves.
   - Optional authorized opponent distribution model, NOT private profiling.

B. Geometry-aware shared encoder:
   - 64-square piece tokens or an efficient board embedding representation;
     alternative: NNUE-like features for lowest latency. Compare experimentally.
   - Context about king safety, pawn structure, piece relationships, target
     square complexes, material, move history.

C. Output heads from ONE shared pass:
   - Human-inspired candidate move policy / one or several diverse policies.
   - Expected value / WDL probability / score estimate.
   - Confidence and calibration / expected value of more search.
   - Future plan, necessary preconditions, likely opposing countermoves.
   - Historical style dimensions and learned independent 'sixth instinct'.
   - Intermediate-layer candidate rescue (only if proven beneficial).

D. Strategic memory:
   - Small latent vector tracking intent, critical prerequisites and what was
     invalidated by the opponent's last move.
   - Re-evaluate, abandon or transform plans when evidence changes.
   - Avoid independent rapid switching between incompatible personalities.

E. Selective computation:
   - Easy position: one fast neural pass and legal-move checks.
   - High-uncertainty or tactically dangerous position: additional candidates,
     shallow/targeted search, or a bounded Stockfish/Leela critic.
   - Asymmetric compute allocation based on actual value of information,
     not arbitrary human-looking delays.

F. Oracle-Heretic system:
   - Stockfish: strongest currently identified lines, objective tactical
     counterargument, MultiPV candidate comparisons, optional evaluation.
   - Heretic/Artist: original but plausible alternatives.
   - Assassin: most dangerous opposing response, refutation.
   - Human Mirror: estimated human choice diversity, only for evaluation and
     legitimate test play, not detector evasion.
   - Historian: plan consistency across the game.
   - Oracle of Regret: estimated loss in expected results if choosing creative
     alternative instead of objective best.
   - Final selection is not simply the highest Stockfish score; evaluate the
     calibrated strength-creativity Pareto frontier.

G. Output:
   - Legal selected move, optionally an interpretable short plan summary.
   - No statement that a verbalized summary necessarily corresponds exactly
     to neural internal reasoning. Faithfulness requires separate tests.

H. Offline training laboratory:
   - Historical PGNs + legitimate annotation rights + source provenance.
   - Deep tactical teacher/search, original motif counterfactual data.
   - Quality-diversity evolutionary research + self-play + ablation.
   - Expensive world-model planning and diffusion generation if useful.
   - Distill to fast mobile model and verify what is actually retained.

CAUTION: A multi-head model with a Stockfish subprocess is still a chess engine.
It is not a biological mind. Novelty and chess understanding require tests.

06. COMPLETE NUMBERED INVENTION CATALOG: ORIGINAL IDEAS 01-45
================================================================================

All following entries are DESIGN PROPOSALS. The number is a conversation-design
identifier; not a patent, publication, implementation, verified unique invention,
or a mapping to any UberBond idea identifier.

01. FUTURE SCULPTOR
    Envision valuable configurations several moves into the future and plan
    backward toward them. Train with verified reachable target positions;
    do not generate fantasies without legal paths. Runtime: small plan head.

02. PARALLEL IMAGINATION CHAMBER
    Attacking, positional, tactical, experimental and skeptical expert
    perspectives propose and challenge plans. Distill debate into shared
    representation; don't run five full models by default on device.

03. DELAYED REVELATION
    Seek plans composed of unthreatening preparatory moves whose purpose
    emerges later. Verify coordination and necessity, not merely surprise.

04. STRATEGIC METAMORPHOSIS ENGINE
    Learn to transform between eccentric opening, restrictive middlegame,
    tactical attack and technical ending, based on positional evidence.

05. ORACLE OF REGRET
    Estimate opportunity cost of a creative move versus an objectively
    stronger alternative using calibrated WDL, not just centipawns.

06. BEAUTIFUL REFUTATION GENERATOR
    Produce attractive but unsound combinations during offline training,
    discover strongest defenses, and learn exact failure conditions.

07. DISAGREEMENT LABORATORY
    Save positions where intuitive policy, causal planner and Stockfish
    diverge; label causes of disagreement, train on useful exceptions.

08. COUNTERFACTUAL BLACKSMITH
    Modify the *earlier* position that prevented a sacrifice from working;
    learn to create those preconditions through playable moves.

09. TEMPTATION CARTOGRAPHER
    Model likely response distribution of strong human players from lawful
    historical/consented data. Scientifically test practical difficulty;
    avoid personal private profiling or enforcement-evasion objectives.

10. HYDRA ARCHITECT
    Construct positions with multiple independent, mutually reinforcing
    threats or latent plans; prefer redundancy over single fragile attacks.

11. COUNTERINTUITIVE POSITION GENERATOR
    Discover novel yet defensible position families and the route to them.
    Test objectively against strong defense and diverse opponents.

12. FALSE-SAFETY RESEARCHER
    Study positions that look comfortable to humans but demand subtle
    calculation; test assumption using controlled human study.

13. ATTENTION-CONFLICT MODEL
    Find positions requiring attention to unrelated strategic fronts,
    e.g. king danger plus distant passed pawn. Test whether this truly
    raises error rates, rather than assuming complexity equals difficulty.

14. PSYCHOLOGICAL COMPLEXITY ATLAS
    Index position classes by observed human error rates, response entropy,
    unfamiliarity, time and actual chess difficulty; offline asset.

15. STRATEGIC AUTOBIOGRAPHY
    Track compact intent across moves: the chosen square complex, reason
    for pawn pushes, threats, what changed; generated prose is secondary.

16. CREATIVE OBSESSION
    Allow strong learned preference for beautiful plans, sometimes leading
    to naturally imperfect overcommitment, not scripted blunder schedules.

17. METACOGNITIVE GOVERNOR
    Predict confidence, tactical danger, novelty and expected value of
    additional thought; allocate actual compute adaptively.

18. MUSEUM OF GLORIOUS FAILURES
    Catalog characteristic flawed ideas and the misconceptions behind them;
    learn generalizable corrective concepts.

19. SIXTH INSTINCT NURSERY / UNBORN GRANDMASTER
    Train an independent expert on positions where historical styles
    disagree; reward verified strong, strategically distinct third options.

20. STYLE GENOME
    Continuous latent axes for initiative, restraint, structural oddity,
    risk, long-term orientation, defensive imagination and tactical taste.

21. CHESS AESTHETIC CRITIC
    Learn from high-quality human expert judgments of beauty, surprise,
    depth, sacrifice, coordination and quiet-move elegance. Calibrate bias.

22. ORIGINALITY AUDITOR
    Compare proposals against historical corpus and standard engine lines;
    novelty must be paired with soundness and out-of-sample transfer.

23. EVOLUTIONARY TOURNAMENT
    Develop policy descendants with distinct traits; objectively select
    useful behaviors against diverse opponents and distill back into core.

24. DREAM LABORATORY
    Generate unfamiliar strategic puzzles and hypothetical states OFFLINE;
    validate reachability, legality, soundness and novelty before training.

25. SEARCH-TO-INSTINCT DISTILLATION
    Train a neural model to predict conclusions of expensive verified search
    in one/few forward passes; compare distillation gains against search cost.

26. TACTICAL DNA RECOMBINATION
    Recombine causal building blocks of famous motifs (open lines, pinned
    defenders, weak squares, exchange compensation) into new verified motifs.

27. MISSING INGREDIENT DETECTOR
    Compare near-identical positions where an idea works/fails; identify
    necessary board features and possible ways to create them.

28. COMPRESSED IMAGINATION LIBRARY
    Distill millions of verified offline discoveries into neural weights,
    optionally retain a small structure-based cache.

29. STRATEGIC MUTATION OPERATOR
    Mutate plans during training (rook lift -> bishop shift, side switch,
    structure modification); keep only robust variants.

30. UNSEEN MOTIF HUNTER
    Quality-diversity archiving: cluster verified unusual ideas by chess
    mechanism to avoid rediscovering the same novelty repeatedly.

31. THREE-FUTURES INSTINCT
    From one shared encoder predict tactical attack, positional conversion,
    and favorable endgame prospects; avoid three complete networks.

32. QUIET-MOVE GENIUS
    Explicitly learn stunning non-check, non-capture, non-threat positional
    choices; prevent the model equating creativity with sacrifices alone.

33. POSITIONAL PARADOX DETECTOR
    Discover when conventionally unattractive structural traits provide
    important dynamic compensation; require causal explanation/test.

34. BEAUTY-FROM-DEFENSE GENERATOR
    Train on remarkable defensive moves, counter-sacrifices, fortress
    ideas, unexpected resources; preserve artistry under pressure.

35. SINGLE-PASS MULTI-INSTINCT HEADS
    One expensive shared inference, several small experts/heads; selective
    routing of candidate styles instead of running full expert models.

36. INCREMENTAL CHESS PERCEPTION
    Reuse board feature computations between moves where architecture
    permits, inspired by incremental NNUE updates; validate correctness.

37. INSTANT REGRET ESTIMATOR
    Cheap learned approximation of how much objective win probability a
    creative candidate sacrifices, with optional deeper critic triggered.

38. SPARSE IMAGINATION ACTIVATION
    Call enhanced creative mechanisms only on high-value or high-uncertainty
    moves; benchmark gating errors and compute savings.

39. SUPPRESSED GENIUS DETECTOR
    Examine intermediate neural layers for promising alternatives lost
    before final policy; train low-cost probes and check whether extracted
    candidate moves have actual tactical quality. Research-inspired,
    but meaningful intermediate decoding is NOT automatic.

40. CAUSAL CHESS IMAGINATION ENGINE
    Formalize future objective -> prerequisites -> refutations -> actions
    to establish prerequisites. Train using causal contrasts and legal
    reachability, NOT only attractive target-state pictures.

41. STRATEGIC TENSION COMPOSER
    Learn when to accumulate, transform or release positional tension;
    dynamic positional complexity as a controlled resource. Distinguish
    causal advantage from metric artifacts.

42. PERSONALITY CONTINUITY MECHANISM
    Persistent strategic latent state prevents incoherent abrupt switching
    between master-style advisors; update or abandon plan on evidence.

43. EVOLUTIONARY MUSEUM OF UNBORN STRATEGIES
    Offline quality-diversity generation of previously unclassified robust
    strategic concepts; archive, audit originality and distill discoveries.

44. CHESS INTUITION AT THREE TIMESCALES
    Shared encoder + immediate threats, medium-term strategic trajectories,
    and long-term structural/precondition prediction heads.

45. EXPERT'S MISTAKE LABORATORY
    Matched successful/failed strategic plans reveal model misconceptions.
    Target calibrated fallibility rather than random errors.

07. OTHER IMPORTANT UNNUMBERED MECHANISMS AND TERMINOLOGY
================================================================================

THE PARADOX WEAVER:
  Positions that appear absurd or contradictory under standard positional
  heuristics but contain a coherent, verified strategic purpose.

THE INVERSE CHESS ENGINE:
  Instead of only 'position -> candidate move -> future', also use
  'desired future -> constraints -> preparation -> actionable move'.

THE ORACLE-HERETIC ENGINE:
  Stockfish as objective critic and refutation oracle; neural original
  creative proposal remains distinct. Oracle should expose strongest enemy
  replies, not just score the top line. The 'worst move' requested by founder
  is most useful as worst PLAUSIBLE candidate or most dangerous refutation,
  not trivially the literal worst legal move.

COGNITIVE DISAGREEMENT ENGINE:
  Preserve differences between intuition, strategic prediction and search;
  differences are opportunities for learning, not evidence of correctness.

NEURAL-ORACLE INDEPENDENCE TEST:
  Compare PURE NEURAL, NEURAL + ORACLE, DISTILLED NEURAL as three agents;
  examine strength, style coherence, creativity, mistake mechanism and speed.

COGNITIVE TURING / NEURAL AUTHENTICITY LABORATORY:
  Disclosed offline/blinded research in which consenting titled experts
  assess games from humans, conventional engines and CHIMERA. Measure
  judgment accuracy, reasons, strategic coherence, error type, not success
  at evading a platform's confidential enforcement system.

CREATIVE INTELLIGENCE COMPILER:
  Train many offline specialist 'teachers' and distill useful disagreements,
  failures and concepts into a smaller mobile student.

STRATEGIC AUTOBIOGRAPHY + FAITHFUL INTERPRETABILITY:
  Explanations should correspond to genuinely predictive latent plan states;
  generated plausible stories alone are insufficient.

OPPONENT MODELING:
  Model population-level human choice distributions for objective practical
  difficulty research; earlier Swindlefish discussion mentioned possible
  negative empirical utility, so require benefit tests.

PARETO FRONTIER:
  Compare candidate moves in objective expected score, practical difficulty,
  originality, beauty, and plan coherence; do not sacrifice substantial
  winning chances merely for unusual-looking moves.

STRATEGIC SHAPE-SHIFTING:
  Different master-inspired instincts activate in different phases, while
  strategic memory stops random personality switching.

NATURAL FALLIBILITY:
  Tunnel vision, false intuition, overconfidence, calculation blind spots,
  plan stubbornness, and creative overreach as consequences of limited
  modeling, not synthetic conspicuous blunders.

09. TRAINING DESIGN: HOW TO CONSTRUCT THE NEW CHESS MIND

STAGE 0: DATA AND REPRODUCIBILITY

  Train legal-move policy, WDL/value, tactical motifs, board relationships,

  Compare a simple NNUE-like baseline and board transformer backbone.

STAGE 2: HUMAN STYLE WITHOUT CONFUSING IT WITH STRENGTH
  Player embeddings and conditioning; predict style residuals controlling
  for Elo, era, opening and opponent. Separate style fidelity and objective
  chess quality. Evaluate unseen games from same and new players.

STAGE 3: CAUSAL TACTICS AND PRECONDITIONS
  Generate matched near-identical positions where a plan works/fails;
  use search to label whether specific pieces/squares/refutations matter.
  Learn 'missing ingredient' representation and plan feasibility.

STAGE 4: COUNTERFACTUAL FUTURES AND CREATIVE DIVERSITY
  Search for unfamiliar but robust motif families and plausible actions
  leading there. Strongest-defense checks, original concepts archive,
  quality-diversity and self-play without rewarding bad novelty.

STAGE 5: ADAPTIVE META-REASONING
  Predict failure risk of initial intuition, expected search improvement,
  plan uncertainty, and compute budget. Store plan state. Test if deeper
  thinking actually improves choices at the same total compute cost.

STAGE 6: ORACLE ADVERSARIAL TEACHER
  Strong Stockfish and/or Leela analysis: MultiPV, tactical refutations,
  win-probability regret, critical positions, negative examples. Teacher
  must not dictate every move or style collapses.

  Distill research ensemble into 50M, 150M, 300M etc. students as feasible;
  retain a separate smaller fast baseline. Quantize. Benchmark on device.

STAGE 8: INDEPENDENT GAMING AND ARTISTIC VALIDATION
  Play disclosed engine tournaments under identical hardware and time
  controls, use expert reviews of originality, and causal tests. Evaluate
  against equal-strength but conventional policies; not only Stockfish.

STAGE 9: FALSIFICATION AND PRUNING
  Remove every new subsystem in turn. If a simpler policy+search matches
  results, delete ornamental complexity. Measure genuine return per watt,

11. EVALUATION, FALSIFIERS, AND SCIENTIFIC DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

DIMENSION A: REAL CHESS STRENGTH
  Elo determined under a specific reproducible pool, clock, hardware and
  pairing protocol. Use confidence intervals; distinguish engine Elo from
  human FIDE classical and online blitz scales.

DIMENSION B: CREATIVE QUALITY
  Blind expert judgment of coherent plans, surprising but sound moves,
  quiet positional brilliance, defensive creativity, and aesthetic depth.
  Control for equal playing strength and board difficulty.

DIMENSION C: INDEPENDENT ORIGINALITY
  New motif clusters relative to training set, grandmaster examples and
  strong baseline engine lines. Novel doesn't imply useful or sound.

DIMENSION D: CAUSAL STRATEGIC COMPETENCE
  Matched positions where one feature invalidates a plan. Test whether
  CHIMERA changes move/plan in the predicted direction for the correct
  tactical or positional reason. Use intervention and counterfactuals.

DIMENSION E: PERSISTENT PLAN COHERENCE
  Does strategic plan state predict future choices? Does CHIMERA revise
  its plans appropriately when adversary disrupts them?

DIMENSION F: BOUNDED COGNITION AND NATURAL ERROR
  Examine categories of mistakes and neural confidence calibration.
  Randomly inserted weak moves or cosmetic timing do NOT qualify.

DIMENSION G: REAL-TIME PHONE PERFORMANCE
  Cold vs warm initialization; per-move p50/p95/p99 and throughput;
  continuous 1h+ battery/thermal behavior; memory peak; CPU/NPU reliability.

DIMENSION H: OPPORTUNITY COST
  Does each clever subsystem improve strength, originality or execution
  enough to justify extra latency, training cost and complexity?

KEY ABLATIONS:
  A0: Plain strong compact policy + standard search (minimal baseline).
  A1: A0 + master style conditioning only.
  A2: A1 + future/causal planning.
  A3: A2 + strategic persistent memory.
  A4: A3 + suppressed-genius intermediate proposals.
  A5: A4 + separate Oracle/Regret controller.
  A6: Full offline creativity discovery + distilled student.

MEASURE:
  - Elo with uncertainty, not selected highlight games.
  - Strongest-defense evaluation; tactical blunder rate by position class.
  - Blind originality scores with inter-rater reliability.
  - Novel verified motifs per million training positions.
  - Counterfactual-plan success rate on held-out structures.
  - Latency, temperature, and energy per move on OnePlus 15.
  - Styles blending vs coherent novel identity.
  - Training and data costs, and ease of reproduction.

STRONGEST FAIR FALSIFIERS:
  1. Stockfish/LC0 at equal latency wins easily while being equally creative
     by blinded expert ratings: CHIMERA is ornamental complexity.
  2. Master-style mixing reduces each style's distinctiveness and creates
     strategic incoherence: mixture is inferior to one policy.
  3. 'Causal' model uses superficial correlations and cannot generalize
     to mutated unseen board positions.
  4. Historical 'new' idea is a memorized position or widely known motif.
  5. Neurally generated human-style errors become far too frequent to retain
     elite strength; risk/creativity frontier is unfavorable.
  6. Mobile GPU/NPU launch overhead, heat or memory stalls swamp gains from
     large model size or MoE routing.
  7. Stockfish critic dominates all move selections, making CHIMERA a style
     wrapper and refuting its claimed independent creativity.
  8. Rarity and human difficulty are different: unusual position families
     fail to increase human errors or win rates in consented studies.

APPENDIX B. ARCHITECTURE: THE FINAL SYNTHESIS WE ARRIVED AT
================================================================================

B.1. PROJECT IDENTITY AND FIRST-PRINCIPLES OBJECTIVE

Project: CHIMERA / The Paradox Weaver / The Sixth Instinct / Causal Creativity
Engine. Experimental, standalone, fully offline neural chess player intended to
operate on an owner-specified OnePlus 15 (16 GB physical RAM; Snapdragon 8 Elite
Gen 5). Inspiration comes from historically inventive masters, but its intended
intelligence is not an imitation classifier or a Stockfish wrapper.

The essential objective is NOT 'play unusual moves'. It is:

  * Generate defensible, counterintuitive candidate plans.
  * Maintain a coherent strategic objective across many plies.
  * Construct missing prerequisites for combinations rather than expect the
    opponent to cooperate with a tactical vision.
  * Recognize, repair or abandon failed ideas.
  * Combine the restraint of Petrosian, the tactical imagination of Tal and
    Nezhmetdinov, the offbeat geometry of Suttles/Basman, the inventive paths
    of Planinc/Morozevich/Dubov, and the adaptability of Ivanchuk.
  * Become independently original through verified learning and evolution.
  * Remain strong against accurate defense, including tactical verification.
  * Offer fast response on the actual phone with quantitative latency/thermal
    evidence rather than marketing assumptions.

A chess neural network generating moves remains a CHESS ENGINE, even when it
produces unusually coherent, nonstandard or psychologically difficult games.
No architectural choice by itself proves a human-like cognitive process or
undetectability by a proprietary classifier.

B.2. PROPOSED MINIMAL RUNTIME PIPELINE

    LEGAL POSITION + EXACT RULES/HISTORY + TIME CONTROL (IF APPLICABLE)
             |
      Geometry-aware board encoder / pretrained chess policy
             |
      Shared contextual representation + compact strategic memory state
             |
    +--------+---------------------+-------------------------------+
    |                              |                               |
    v                              v                               v
  Creative Candidate Head     Causal Plan Head              Value/Uncertainty Head
  - strong usual move         - goal                         - expected result
  - surprising viable move    - preconditions               - calibration
  - defensive resource        - refutations                 - fragility
  - possible latent salvage   - exit/recovery               - value of thinking
    |                              |                               |
    +------------------------------+-------------------------------+
                                   |
                     Plan compatibility reconciler
                                   |
               Selective mini-search / objective Oracle (optional)
                                   |
                      Legal, calibrated move selection
                                   |
                      Updated memory + research receipt

This is a REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE, not a claim that any specific stock model
implements all heads. The goal is one major backbone inference per routine move,
with optional additional thinking only where its expected benefit is positive.
The best design might use simple search instead of a sophisticated auxiliary
head. Baseline comparisons decide.

B.3. SEPARATE OFFLINE EXPENSIVE TRAINING FROM PHONE INFERENCE

Training-time components can be expensive, numerous and research-oriented:

    A. PGN acquisition, legality/deduplication, provenance, time controls,
       chronological split and player/style annotations.
    B. Strong objective teachers: Stockfish, Leela/BT4, tablebases (when legal
       and feasible), MultiPV, best defense and search-gap comparisons.
    C. Human decision teachers: Maia-3, style-conditioned models, Otter,
       ChessMimic, CHSM8, historical human databases.
    D. Reasoning teachers: Queen-style explanation research, causal concept
       graphs, plan-step process reward models, verified motif extraction.
    E. Discovery: counterfactual boards, diffusion/latent plans, quality-diversity
       evolutionary leagues, concept vectors and SAE interventions.
    F. Conflict resolution: opponent-resistance models, compatibility graphs,
       adversarial evaluations, synthetic plan failures and negative examples.
    G. Distillation: encode only validated, reusable and complementary
       discoveries into a lightweight or moderate-sized mobile student.

Runtime should NOT continuously execute every research tool, SAE analyzer,
physics-style topology solver, giant language model, diffusion model and teacher.
It should execute the capabilities that proved their marginal benefit.

B.4. THE THREE-BRAIN PRINCIPLE

 - THE ARTIST: Proposes surprising objectives, unique board transformations,
   tactical opportunities and quiet preconditions. It cannot be reduced to a
   random multiplier applied to Stockfish move scores.
 - THE ARCHITECT: Tracks strategic dependencies across time, prevents the
   original plan from being abandoned accidentally, and detects conflicts
   between goals, defenders, squares and tempos.
 - THE SKEPTIC/ORACLE: Attempts to disprove the idea with the strongest
   counterplay. It may use searched WDL, tactical refutations, and calibrated
   confidence. Strong defense is a hard scientific constraint, not an aesthetic
   preference.

All three are LOGICAL roles, not necessarily separate compute-heavy models.
A shared encoder with small heads may implement them.

B.5. PERSONALITY MUST BE CAUSAL AND DYNAMIC

Historical player embeddings should not dictate a simplistic 'style blend' at
every move. A current plan may need one of several moves, and the best idea may
be uncharacteristic of every teacher. Persistent intent includes:

  plan_id, target_square_complex, objective_class, supporting_motifs,
  board_preconditions, refutation_candidates, repair_options,
  confidence/calibration, previous_plan_context, update_reason.

This is an explanatory design schema, not final serialization. All state can be
represented as a small quantized latent and/or sparse concept indices. The
exact legal history, repetition, fifty-move clock, side to move and castling
rights are separately held by deterministic chess logic.

B.6. THE 'SUPERHUMAN KNOWLEDGE, ORIGINAL ART' DISTINCTION

CHIMERA can learn strong tactics from objective engines without becoming an
engine that always chooses the current oracle move. The research aim is to
internalize: when to sacrifice; why sacrifices fail; which conditions can be
constructed; when to suppress counterplay; and which unusual but sound plans
would otherwise be omitted by conventional move-probability collapse.

To retain natural fallibility (rather than manufactured errors), investigate:
finite representational capacity, selective calculation, uncertainty, plan
commitment, imperfect learned strategic values, false pattern matching and
failure to notice a rare defense. Study errors as an emergent property of bounded
reasoning. Avoid treating scripted blunder rates as proof of cognition.

B.7. PROFILE LAYER IS DISTINCT FROM COGNITION

The founder also designed a Profile/BandConfig layer for selecting among known
candidates without modifying search/eval internals. It is useful for offline
sparring, education, reproducible experiments and subjective style tuning.
It does not itself create tactical insight or causal planning, and it is NOT
part of the proof of creative intelligence. See Appendix C for its original
specification and unresolved consistency defects.

B.8. THE MOST COMPELLING RESEARCH FUSION

Stage 1: Historical masters' games teach a vocabulary of chess motifs.
Stage 2: Search-gap + CQL-style counterfactuals mine surprising but defensible
         plans and identify the conditions that make them work.
Stage 3: Plan-step PRM and adversarial critic assess coherence and resilience.
Stage 4: Learned causal plan bottleneck tracks those conditions across moves.
Stage 5: Quality-diversity experiment searches for a sixth independent style.
Stage 6: Neural interpretability examines whether trained internal features
         causally contribute to these capabilities.
Stage 7: Knowledge distillation + selective search produce a fast on-device
         student while retaining a calibrated objective critic.
Stage 8: Blind artistic evaluations, Elo matches, ablations and phone telemetry
         decide whether the system earns its extra complexity.

C.6. STATISTICAL TEST PROTOCOL

APPENDIX D. EVERY ADVANCED RESEARCH BATCH: ORIGINAL INTENT AND REPAIR

This section preserves the founder's cross-domain speculative theories,
including their distinctive mathematical content. [CLAIM] records what the
founder proposed or prior responses reported. [REPAIR] records scientific
limitations and a testable alternative. Nothing below is proven merely by
being expressible in mathematical notation.

D.1. PROCESS REWARD MODELS FOR PLANS

[FOUNDER] Score 3–5-ply sub-trajectories based on coherent strategic progress,
not only terminal centipawns. Examples: occupation of an outpost, prophylactic
king shift, minority attack, constructive plan persistence despite small
intermediate evaluation dips. Plan-Step PRM should teach cohesive games.

[REPAIR] A plan must be abandonable when refuted. Define a progress metric, a
robustness-to-defense measure, the option value of alternate continuations,
repairability and objective game outcome. Rewards that merely reinforce the
same plan can teach stubbornness. Experiments: explicit PRM vs equal-size value
head, PRM without defense critic, and standard search.

D.2. LATENT DIFFUSION / MASKED ROLLOUT PLANNING

[FOUNDER] Non-autoregressive diffusion over 6–8 plies, conditioned on an
intended future board state; iterative masking recovers legal intervening
moves. Proposal embodies the Inverse Chess Engine as a generative path model.

[REPAIR] One attractive line cannot assume the adversary cooperates. Include
branching opponent responses, legal-board validation, and strongest-defense
checks. Full iterative diffusion likely costs multiple passes; a stronger
candidate is use as an offline teacher, then distill into a one-pass plan
head. Research lead: 'Implicit Search via Discrete Diffusion: A Study on Chess'
(arXiv:2502.19805), as reported in prior discussion.

D.3. COMPLEXITY-SCALED BOUNDED RATIONALITY

[FOUNDER] Use legal-move branching entropy and spread of WDL among candidates
to adjust the amount of risk or calculation. Separate quiet, intuitive chess
from chaotic tactical scrambles; allow naturally limited tactical processing.

[REPAIR] Best-worst value spread is not a sufficient complexity measure.
Policy disagreement, value instability with added search and discovery cost of
an actual refutation are more informative. A position with only two viable
moves may be harder than a position with forty legal moves. Use calibrated
value-of-thought prediction rather than a hard-coded error schedule.

D.4. ADVERSARIAL SEARCH-GAP MINING

[FOUNDER] Mine counterintuitive moves where shallow search initially dislikes
them and deeper search validates them. Proposed illustrative cutoff included
DeepEval(22) minus ShallowEval(5) > 0.8 pawns; preference-train uncommon moves
via DPO. Expand to quiet moves, exchange sacrifices, prophylaxis and fortresses.

[REPAIR] Search-depth results are not interchangeable across engines/settings;
a large gap may be a shallow engine defect, a horizon artifact, or an opponent
blunder, not a strategically reusable concept. Mine BOTH true and false
reversals, use common root search budgets and robust independent verification.
Best experiments compare DPO against SFT and conventional policy search on
held-out counterintuitive positions. Research lead: NeurIPS 2025 creative
puzzle generation; previous reported rates 0.22% to 2.5% are study-specific.

D.5. NEURO-SYMBOLIC CQL MOTIF INJECTION

[FOUNDER] Use Chess Query Language to label pins, interference, overloading,
zugzwang, pawn lever/undermining, opposite-color-bishop attacks and other
motifs; condition the neural chess brain on these symbolic patterns.

[REPAIR] Distinguish 'motif present', 'motif strategically relevant' and
'motif can be deliberately created in legal play'. Merely detecting a pin
should not reward pursuing it. CQLi's hypothetical-position tools provide an
offline source of useful counterfactual queries, but reachability and objective
quality still require chess validation.

D.6. SAE CONCEPT SURGERY / ACTIVATION STEERING

[FOUNDER] Train sparse autoencoders on residual streams; extract sparse chess
features associated with king-cage geometry, prophylaxis, long-range exchange
compensation and unusual pawn sacrifices. Steer activation vectors directly
inside the inference network instead of changing output move probabilities.
Illustrative layer ranges 19–23 were proposed without proof of compatibility
with every checkpoint.

[REPAIR] Correlation of a sparse feature with a concept does not prove causality,
nor do SAEs guarantee disentangled chess concepts. Interventions may be
unstable, expensive or entangled with unrelated tactical capacities. Test
matched positions, placebo vectors, held-out chess patterns, and model size.
Fine-tune and distill useful effects; do not claim 'zero overhead'. Reported
late-2026 research leads on 'Playing Dumb' need a fresh source audit before
using numeric claims as established engineering requirements.

D.7. ACTIVE INFERENCE / EPISTEMIC CHESS

[FOUNDER] Separate pragmatic expected result from cognitive challenge to the
opponent; seek positions with high opponent response uncertainty but strong
objective chances. Framed as active inference/free-energy minimization in a
perfect-information game with unequal human perception capacities.

[REPAIR] Chess has no hidden board state. Uncertainty concerns modeled reply
preferences, not board knowledge. Response entropy, information gain and
strategic difficulty are different statistical quantities. A model may be
uncertain because several good moves exist; that is not necessarily hard.
Define and separately calibrate objective WDL, strongest defense, observed
human-response distribution, practical difficulty and model epistemic error.

D.8. TOPOLOGICAL PERSISTENCE / STRATEGIC TENSION MANIFOLDS

[FOUNDER] Build weighted directed attack/defense/X-ray/pin interaction graphs,
calculate persistent homology over short future rollouts and reward persistent
multi-piece tension or strategic cycles. Hypothesis: preserve unresolved
coordination until a decisive phase transition.

[REPAIR] A high Betti number does not imply chess strength; many advantageous
exchanges appropriately simplify the graph. First test simpler and cheaper
graph summaries against baseline board neural networks. Persistent homology
belongs in an offline feature-discovery ablation until independently useful.

D.9. VERBALIZATION-GROUNDED SYMBOLIC BOTTLENECK

[FOUNDER] Distill tactical search/LLM explanations into a compact symbolic
intent representing goal, defender, target complex and trigger; require move
prediction to depend on the encoded plan. Keep phone inference free of a
large text-generating model. Prior discussion cited Queen (arXiv:2610.03695)
as a reasoning/explanation teacher.

[REPAIR] Fluent explanation is not faithful control. Intervene on the encoded
plan while keeping board fixed; test whether move selection changes in
appropriate, chess-sound ways. Use invalidation and fallback conditions;
retain exact game rules independent of latent memory.

D.10. QUALITY-DIVERSITY ALIEN-CONCEPT EVOLUTION

[FOUNDER] Run self-play evolutionary specialists and mine machine concepts
outside historical GM clusters, initially framed as M\\H. Use SAE concepts,
search gaps and database novelty to reward original strategy. Distill the
successful discoveries into a mobile student.

[REPAIR] Latent orthogonality to a historical corpus is not proof of novelty,
meaning or quality. It may just detect odd openings or artifacts. Use verified
strength, strategic distinctness, out-of-distribution transfer, and annotated
corpus novelty separately. Preserve provenance before claiming any new chess
concept has no historical precedent.

D.11. VQ-MDL / KOLMOGOROV-BOUNDED STRATEGIC PROGRAM

[FOUNDER] Compress an intended six-ply future into a discrete 16-bit intent
code, purportedly making incoherent or alien move sequences impossible.

[REPAIR] Kolmogorov complexity is not computable as an ordinary training metric.
A 16-bit code holds 65,536 categories, not necessarily 65,536 meaningful plans.
A single fixed bottleneck could remove essential tactical information. Prefer
variable-length discrete plan tokens, explicit compression regularization,
causal evidence tests and an emergency tactical path outside the plan code.
A compact code cannot logically prohibit all disconnected move sequences.

Candidate formal objective:

  L_total = L_chess + alpha L_plan + beta L_rate + gamma L_robustness

where rates, plan faithfulness and objective chess outcomes are all defined
and independently measurable; variable coefficients are hyperparameters,
not universal constants.

D.12. PREDICTIVE CODING / WAKE-SLEEP / SURPRISE

[FOUNDER] Predict the opponent's next replies, update on unexpected moves and
trigger deeper computation when prediction error spikes. Proposed an automatic
brief 'cognitive shock' and recovery window following surprise.

[REPAIR] Observed move surprisal -log P(a|history) differs from Bayesian
information gain D_KL(posterior || prior). One need not lower competence
whenever surprised; a competent player can focus more effectively. Best use
is a surprise-attribution head that identifies which plan assumptions changed
and recalculates those parts. The claimed exact post-surprise dip is not
established by current source evidence.

D.13. CONTRASTIVE SAEs / WORKING-MEMORY ABLATION

[FOUNDER] Contrast successful long-range plans with tactical illusions, find
sparse internal features representing lookahead and pattern recognition, then
attenuate deep computation based on position complexity.

[REPAIR] Do not assume a one-to-one physical distinction between 'superhuman
lookahead circuit' and 'human pattern circuit'. A late layer may encode
multiple concepts. Attenuation may cause uncalibrated errors or lose strong
tactical play. Test causal probes, competency-specific interventions and
model-calibrated routing on exact checkpoint architecture.

D.14. BILEVEL THEORY OF MIND / PERCEPTUAL OPPONENT POSTERIOR

[FOUNDER] Use a learned opponent perceptual policy as an inner optimization
and a CHIMERA move policy as an outer optimizer. Hypothesis: find positions
whose objective nature conflicts with an opponent's intuitive predictions.

[REPAIR] Jensen-Shannon divergence is defined between comparable probability
distributions, not between a scalar centipawn score and a policy directly.
Objective best defense remains an important constraint. Opponent response
models need held-out calibration and uncertainty intervals. The scientific
contribution is modeling robust strategic dilemmas, not assuming traps are
objectively sound because a perceptual prior is uncertain.

D.15. FISHER INFORMATION / LEGAL POSITION FRAGILITY

[FOUNDER] Apply the information geometry of the value function to identify
sharp versus forgiving positions. Proposed Fisher information trace from
gradients of a neural value head and geodesic sampling for artistic freedom.

[REPAIR] Gradient norm is not automatically Fisher trace; Fisher information
is not curvature by itself; model embeddings do not represent arbitrary legal
chess transformations. In a Bernoulli outcome model with probability p(x),
a candidate input Fisher trace is ||grad_x p||^2/[p(1-p)], under appropriate
regularity, but x is continuous and legal states are constrained/discrete.
Start by measuring actual value sensitivity to LEGAL continuations and train
an inexpensive predictor. Test any added geometric formalism only if it
improves verified chess prediction above simpler measures.

D.16. HODGE–HELMHOLTZ / DISCRETE EXTERIOR CALCULUS

[FOUNDER] Interpret square/piece interactions as a discrete vector field,
decompose edge 1-form into gradient, curl and harmonic subspaces, and use
harmonic restraint cycles to guide Petrosian-style non-forcing pressure.

[REPAIR] On an appropriate simplicial complex, omega = B1^T phi + B2 psi + h
is a meaningful Hodge decomposition. But a fully filled planar board has
trivial first homology, and the harmonic part need not capture chess restraint.
Existing engines do not only 'see gradient'; their search and neural values
already represent long-range relationships. Build a valid chess-interaction
complex, test whether structural Hodge features improve results compared
with graph features, and treat the 'restraint' interpretation as hypothesis.

D.17. RENORMALIZATION / MERA MULTISCALE CHESS

[FOUNDER] Treat tactical details and strategic pawn/king structures as
microscopic and macroscopic scales, using RG/MERA-inspired coarse graining to
identify moves capable of triggering structural transitions many plies later.

[REPAIR] Quantum MERA scaling operators do not mathematically guarantee a
chess pawn push will lead to a forced later collapse. The useful hypothesis is
hierarchical board perception and tactical-to-strategic information transfer.
Start with a multiscale graph transformer and compare to standard board
encoders at matched parameters, memory and latency.

D.18. RATE–DISTORTION / INFORMATION BOTTLENECK

[FOUNDER] Model finite cognitive information processing as lossy compression;
proposed Blahut-Arimoto optimization over board information and policy choice,
including a suggested 10–15-bit/sec conscious channel capacity.

[REPAIR] There is no validated universal human chess-processing rate of that
form; information theory alone does not guarantee human-like error types.
A meaningful training objective is maximize expected chess utility minus
beta I(S;Z), preserving a compact Z predictive of future winning and plan
constraints. Test whether this helps chess strength per millisecond.

D.19. HAMILTONIAN / SYMPLECTIC CHESS DYNAMICS

[FOUNDER] Model initiative as momentum and chess structures as q,p phase
space, infer a conserved 'strategic energy', use Lyapunov instability to
select Tal-like complications when behind and stable KAM regions when ahead.

[REPAIR] Chess is discrete, adversarial, usually irreversible and not known
to preserve a symplectic form or conserved strategic energy. Captures and
promotion break the physical analogy. Replace with branch-outcome instability
from legal variations and calibrated response policies. Retain the conceptual
question about choosing volatile versus stable positions; do not impose false
conservation laws.

D.20. SHEAF-THEORETIC LOCAL-TO-GLOBAL PLAN CONSISTENCY

[FOUNDER] Decompose the board into overlapping strategic regions; use local
constraints and compatible restriction maps to identify attacking plans that
conflict with defense elsewhere. Proposed cellular/Čech cohomology as cheap
replacement for brute-force tactical verification.

[REPAIR] A local assignment s is compatible if delta^0 s = 0, but delta^0 s
is a 1-cochain, not itself a nontrivial H^1 class. Vanishing H^1 does not
prove a chess move is sound. A small learned compatibility graph might catch
local contradictions in one pass; strong tactical refutation is still needed.
Benchmark against hand-designed constraint graphs and ordinary search.

D.21. DYNAMIC ATTENTION-LAYER RECRUITMENT

[FOUNDER] On a frozen chess transformer, modulate the gain of deeper
attention layers based on board complexity and a claimed skill-conditioning
result. Illustrative draft referred to Maia-3 layers 8–16.

[REPAIR] The previously inspected Maia3-79M model has EIGHT blocks, 0–7.
The cited paper investigated how attention contributions vary with skill
conditioning; it did not prove arbitrary residual gain scaling is zero-cost
or improves decisions. Suppressing deeper processing when tactics are hard
may be precisely backward. Try learned competency-based routing with frozen
checkpoint controls, then distill any reliable gains.

D.22. LATENT RECURRENT REASONING

[FOUNDER] Re-use middle transformer blocks with a learned gate, k=1 for easy
positions and up to k=4–6 or more for difficult positions, thereby obtaining
adaptive 'System 2' reasoning without explicit search nodes.

[REPAIR] Repeating blocks after residual connections can explode or degrade
representations; stable recurrence may require normalization and fine-tuning.
One candidate research recurrence:

  h_(k+1) = Norm(h_k + g_k [F(h_k,h_0) - h_k])

Neither loop count nor a small KL difference between successive policies
proves correctness. Train on initial errors corrected by later computation;
stop based on calibrated expected improvement and latency. Add a strong
search baseline with comparable compute.

[REPAIR] Independently trained models have incompatible coordinate spaces;

[REPAIR] Those timing figures are unmeasured; frozen repeats can degrade

measure policy improvement and wallclock before adopting.

[REPAIR] The full signature's uniqueness theorems require assumptions;
finite level-two signatures over a short window are not an identity proof.
Use as sequence diagnostics for strategic progress and plan repair, not as a
surrogate anti-cheat score or a guarantee of human origin. Research lead:
'Turk and Polson, Chess Signatures of Play' arXiv:2606.18544, as reported.

[FOUNDER] Separate Geometric Attention Bias from dot-product attention and

[REPAIR] The previously discussed 0.005 result described within-position
GAB attention behavior across squares, not proven orthogonality of GAB and
DPA. They are summed inside softmax and interact. A single skill embedding
interpolation does not establish independent DPA/GAB skill coordinates.

[REPAIR] Time conditioning is an empirical predictive feature, not proof

Use exact clock only where relevant; avoid fake clock pauses. For offline

FIDE classical Elo; source authenticity and evaluation require review.

D.34. MAIA3 × LEELA BT4 CROSS-BACKBONE SPLICING

[FOUNDER] Align last-layer residuals of Maia3-79M and bt4-332; combine
strength-oriented and historically informed chess decisions in a single
student. Particular checkpoint named BT4-1024x15x32h-swa-6147500-policytune-332.
The founder asserted near-2600 CElo raw searchless performance for BT4.

[REPAIR] Maia-3 reportedly has 8 blocks; BT4 has 15 and different dimensions,
weights, training distribution and head meaning. Shared square tokenization
is not residual compatibility. Strong BT4 performance without search was
not verified in previous research. Prefer align concepts and distill to a
new student; independently benchmark BT4 raw-policy and search modes.

D.35. SEQUENCE-CONDITIONED HISTORY TRACKING

[FOUNDER] Add persistent Cross-Ply Recurrent State Token to avoid resetting
context every FEN. A reported 2026 paper (arXiv:2603.29761) compared tracking
and deciding, citing 55.2% move prediction, and a 2570 Lichess bullet result.

[REPAIR] Those are study-specific reported numbers, not FIDE equivalent.
Game-history representations must distinguish exact legal history from
learned strategic memory; neural latent state alone cannot enforce castling,
en-passant, threefold repetition or fifty-move rules. Compare same-board
histories to detect real memory value.

D.36. CHESSMIMIC THREE-HEAD MODELING

[FOUNDER] Jointly model move, time and outcome using board/history/rating/
clock conditioning, then apply the practical outcome head to evaluate complex
positions. Prior claimed outcome ROC AUC ~0.777. Suggested using an artificial
clock-lead threshold to shift policy toward drawn-out complications.

[REPAIR] Outcome AUC is not causal evidence that selecting a candidate
improves score. Clock-prediction correlations from earlier research were
moderate. Keep outcome prediction and deliberation research separate; test
actual strength and opponent decision difficulty. No account-behavior
or detector-specific scheduling should be implemented.

D.37. SPARSE HIGH-LEVERAGE THINKING

[FOUNDER] A 'few strong moves' intervention paper (arXiv:2601.05386) was
used to motivate brief stronger computation on a handful of important plies.
Published abstract figures quoted in prior discussion were about expected
score 0.51 baseline, 0.71 for one and 0.82 for two assisted moves in a
specific controlled simulation.

[REPAIR] Do not generalize these outcomes or interpret as a universal number
of decisive moves. For legitimate engine design, turn this into adaptive
computation: where does a tiny extra budget have greatest expected impact?
The original founder also proposed fresh-account rating ramps and concealed
selective model injection; those are preserved as historical context only,
not as a deployment objective.

D.38. SURROGATE FAIR-PLAY CLASSIFIER / ADVERSARIAL FILTER

[FOUNDER] Proposed an ensemble using move/time sequence models and path
signatures to constrain a move generator according to a classifier's idea of
engine-vs-human play. The request was expressed explicitly as optimization
against anti-cheating enforcement and included account-calibration behavior.

[DESIGN BOUNDARY] This is not a component of the final CHIMERA architecture
or an authorized benchmark for platform evasion. Its transferable scientific
question is how to assess latent chess cognition and stylistic coherence with
independent, disclosed, consenting evaluations. See Appendix H. Do not
fabricate proprietary Chess.com detectors or claim access to their thresholds.

D.39. EXPONENTIAL TILTING / KL-REGULARIZED POLICY IMPROVEMENT

[FOUNDER] Proposes logit-space exponential tilting relative to a Maia-3 move
prior, aiming to improve expected chess outcomes without fully replacing the
original policy. A separate motivation targeted detector-distribution matching.

[REPAIR] In KL-regularized control, a common optimizer has form

  pi_lambda(a|s) ∝ pi_0(a|s) * exp(lambda * Q(s,a))

under a properly specified objective with feasible support and normalized
Q. Same support does NOT preserve the original rare-move frequency, and
near-zero KL from a proxy prior does not prove similarity to actual human
move streams. Use regularization to preserve a desirable chess personality
while improving objective strength, not to minimize enforcement detection.

D.40. PRACTICAL OUTCOME CRITIC VERSUS OBJECTIVE VALUE

[FOUNDER] Use a ChessMimic-like expected-score model Q_practical(s,a) in
addition to or instead of Stockfish's objective value, asserting potential
improvement per information-theoretic cost of deviation.

[REPAIR] Keep two distinct calibrated critics: objective best-defense WDL and
actual expected results under a well-specified population/opponent model.
Outcome AUC does not equal calibrated counterfactual action value or a proven
relative reduction in statistical divergence. Train and evaluate separately.

D.41. ADAPTIVE INFORMATION BUDGET / WATER-FILLING

[FOUNDER] Vary the strength of regularization according to decision
complexity, attempting to spend less on routine positions and more on
strategically pivotal moments. Original equations also targeted a maximum
log-likelihood ratio against a human detector baseline.

[REPAIR] Adapt computation according to *expected improvement in decision
quality* and resource cost. Avoid guessing an enforcement threshold or
statistical profile. A simple forced move requires no elaborate policy
optimization; a sharply disputed quiet move may merit deeper review.

D.42. KL-REGULARIZED MINI-LOOKAHEAD

[FOUNDER] Small search (illustrative 16–32 nodes) regularized toward a Maia
chess prior, supposedly under 40 ms on OnePlus and gaining 150–200 Elo.

[REPAIR] KL-regularized search has published precedent (e.g., Jacob et al.,
ICML 2022); cited improvement values and mobile costs are unverified here.
Use a mini-search to challenge creative candidate refutations and to retain
personality only when chess soundness allows. Benchmark at equal actual
compute against regular search and stronger one-pass student models.

D.43. JOINT MOVE/TIME DISTRIBUTIONS

[FOUNDER] Joint modeling of move and move time; stated conditional KL time
term could become zero with a matching clock model, and proposed conditioning
on candidate move for detection-signature control.

[REPAIR] KL chain rule is correct for consistently defined true joint
probabilities, but a learned clock model is not the true grandmaster time
conditional. The divergence would not automatically vanish. For legitimate
research, predict actual cognitive effort and measure latency/energy, not
artificial time distributions designed to mask engine assistance.

APPENDIX E. MATHEMATICAL CAUTIONS AND FALSIFIERS
================================================================================

1. 'One-of-one' and 'never built' are novelty hypotheses; prior art can
   invalidate them even when an implementation is excellent.
2. The 64-square format is a tokenization choice, not evidence that all
   Chessformer-like networks have compatible residual coordinate systems.
3. Logit similarity, feature decodability and verbal explanation do not prove
   identical reasoning, true human cognition, or causal strategic planning.
4. A single carefully chosen line from a generator is not an adversarial
   continuation; strongest opponent response must be evaluated.
5. Human-like-looking mistakes and correct timing are not scientific proof of
   human cognition. Real neural chess systems can have fundamentally
   different latent causal structure.
6. KL regularization trades optimization against a chosen reference policy;
   it does not provide a detector-specific statistical guarantee.
7. Lower candidate entropy can signify certainty or a pathological model;
   high entropy can signify multiple good alternatives, not high difficulty.
8. High graph complexity, persistent cycles or topological invariants do not
   automatically mean strategic compensation.
9. Information geometry requires a clearly defined statistical family,
   coordinate system and metric; derivatives of unconstrained embeddings do
   not magically identify legal tactical sensitivity.
10. A recurrent transformer can stabilize on a bad move. Stable policy KL is
    not a sufficient stopping criterion.
11. A compressed symbolic plan may be a decorative label. Causal plan tests
    require controlled interventions and successful predictions.
12. A learned reward model can be gamed. Objective game results, adversarial
    validation and independent human judgments should constrain beauty and
    coherence rewards.
13. Search-depth comparisons need consistent software, WDL calibration,
    depth/node accounting and side-to-move conventions.
14. Strong style transfer claims need held-out games, opponent and era
    controls, not merely famous-player labels.
15. No win-rate, Elo, originality, phone-latency or thermal improvement may
    be reported as a result until observed in a reproducible experiment.

APPENDIX F. TRAINING PROGRAM: AN IMPLEMENTABLE RESEARCH SEQUENCE
================================================================================

F.0. GOVERNING RESEARCH HYPOTHESES

H1. Distinct historical style can be learned after accounting for chess
strength, era, opening and position class.
H2. Counterfactual prerequisites add useful creativity relative to a search-
only policy at matched computation.
H3. Plan-state/PRM machinery produces independently recognizable coherent
multi-move strategies without sacrificing a large amount of playing strength.
H4. Distillation preserves some of the benefits of expensive creative teachers
on a small or medium phone-side student.
H5. Concept/circuit interventions have causal, replicable effects instead of
mere post-hoc labels.
H6. Human-opponent difficulty modeling yields measurable benefits only if it
survives strongest-defense chess and reproduces on new players.
H7. Latent recurrence improves decision quality only in a subset of positions;
a learned value-of-thought predictor can identify that subset better than
unconditional repeated passes.
H8. The final student outperforms simpler equal-inference-budget baselines on
pre-registered creative and strength benchmarks. The default null hypothesis
is that it does NOT.

F.1. DATA PROVENANCE AND SPLIT CONTROLS

Acquisition:
 - Full legally usable PGNs from accessible sources; copy licensing metadata.
 - Label player, game date, Elo/rating system, time control, event, result,
   source URLs and whether score is a complete legal game.
 - Deduplicate by normalized moves as well as by header/title; handle mirrors,
   fragments, transpositions and annotated alternate lines separately.
 - Verify every position through a deterministic chess rules library.
 - Store checksum of source files and dataset manifest.
 - Exclude duplicate games from both train and validation sets.
 - Partition chronologically and/or by complete games; maintain external test
   sets from rare openings and unseen players.
 - Prevent teacher leakage: Stockfish lines in training must not also be used
   as untouched truth for evaluation.
 - Distinguish blitz/bullet/rapid/classical; do not merge these into one
   'human rating' dataset without calibration.
 - Copyright/source rights matter; software licenses and model weights are
   separately governed.

F.2. BASELINES BEFORE NOVELTY

Baseline A: existing pretrained style-conditioned policy (e.g. Maia-3) with
            legal masking and a neutral selection scheme.
Baseline B: strong searchless chess policy without stylistic intervention.
Baseline C: neural policy + small fixed-budget objective search.
Baseline D: Stockfish/Lc0 strength-limited and standard search baselines.
Baseline E: simple profile-only candidate sampling over a strong engine.
Baseline F: historical master imitation model of comparable size.

Strength test results from one rating pool are NOT interchangeable with human
FIDE classical ratings. Record source, competition time control, hardware,
opening distribution, opponents and confidence intervals for each claim.

F.3. SPECIALIZED EXPERIMENTS

Experiment C-01: STYLE AND PLAYER EMBEDDINGS
  Input: verified PGNs across founder-selected masters, rating controls.
  Models: shared policy, player-tag model, continuous latent style model.
  Test: held-out player move prediction and style classification, equal size.
  Reject if classification only reproduces opening repertoire/era.

Experiment C-02: SEARCH-GAP INTUITION
  Input: shallow/deep evaluated candidates and matched failed ideas.
  Models: base policy, SFT, DPO, DPO with causal concept labels.
  Test: held-out verified unusual candidate recall and performance.
  Reject if novelty rises solely through objectively losing sacrifices.

Experiment C-03: COUNTERFACTUAL BLACKSMITH
  Input: position pairs differing by decisive defending/motif condition.
  Models: standard policy, motif head, causal prerequisite head.
  Test: whether the model creates reachable prerequisites in new games.
  Reject if target position looks attractive but cannot arise vs defense.

Experiment C-04: PLAN-STEP PRM
  Input: short and long plan trajectories, explicit failed plan variants.
  Models: no PRM, outcome-only reward, progress-only, robust plan PRM.
  Test: game-level plan adherence WHEN rational; plan repair after defense.
  Reject if model becomes strategically stubborn or loses more games.

Experiment C-05: CAUSAL BOTTLENECK
  Input: plan witnesses, compressed intent tokens, adversarial positions.
  Models: unconstrained policy, fixed 16-bit VQ, variable-length plan code.
  Test: strength, plan faithfulness, compression ratio and interventions.
  Reject if compression destroys tactical strength or codes have no semantics.

Experiment C-06: INTERPRETABILITY AND CONCEPT GRAFTING
  Input: matched conceptual positions, trained sparse probes/SAEs.
  Models: frozen controls, placebo directions, learned adapter fusion,
          trained distillation student.
  Test: reproducible improvements on new chess classes without regressions.
  Reject if intervention only amplifies existing biases or hurts defense.

Experiment C-07: MULTISCALE / GRAPH / SHEAF FEATURES
  Input: chess interaction graphs and regional plan constraints.
  Models: vanilla square encoder, graph encoding, multiscale graph encoding,
          compatibility graph, mathematical topology extras.
  Test: tactical-transition/plan-conflict accuracy, resulting match scores.
  Reject if simpler features match results or overhead exceeds benefit.

Experiment C-08: SEQUENCE MEMORY
  Input: whole games and matched identical FEN states with varied history.
  Models: no history, last-N moves, recurrent latent state, causal plan state.
  Test: plan coherence, legal rule correctness, game outcomes.
  Reject if history encourages stale assumptions or no positive transfer.

Experiment C-09: ADAPTIVE THOUGHT
  Input: decisions where added compute changes outcome, including false
         confidence and tactical surprise.
  Models: fixed loop/search, entropy gate, disagreement gate,
          calibrated expected-value-of-thinking gate.
  Test: actual Elo per ms/node/joule, P50/P95/P99 move latency.
  Reject if loops converge without correcting mistakes.

Experiment C-10: QUALITY-DIVERSITY SIXTH STYLE
  Input: diverse policy descendants, verified novelty archives.
  Models: simple self-play, player mix, QD league, QD plus concept archive.
  Test: unbeaten/defensible novel motifs and transfer on new opponents.
  Reject if 'novelty' means only rare blunders or unseen transpositions.

Experiment C-11: DUAL CRITIC
  Input: objective search-WDL and independently measured outcome model.
  Models: objective critic only, practical critic only, dual critic.
  Test: objective soundness, opponent results and calibration.
  Reject if practical critic rewards false optimism against weak replies.

Experiment C-12: COMPLETE HUMAN-STYLE RESEARCH STUDY
  Only disclosed, offline, appropriately consented evaluation.
  Blind titled-player reviews comparing masters, CHIMERA, Maia-style
  imitation, searchless policies and conventional engines; ask experts to
  annotate strategic intent, originality and erroneous assumptions.
  Control for actual playing strength and time control. A model being called
  humanlike by a reviewer is NOT evidence of biological cognition.

F.4. MEASUREMENT DEFINITIONS

  Strength: win/draw/loss score and Elo-type estimates with intervals for
  explicit opponent pool and time control. 'Human-equivalent FIDE 2500' is
  not a supported number without human-calibrated comparison.

  Creativity: blind expert ratings for justified novelty, quiet-move quality,
  sacrifice depth, positional inventiveness, defense and whole-game design.
  Normalize ratings and track judge disagreement.

  Objective soundness: strongest-defense engine analysis, WDL regret under
  comparable budgets, successful defense against unfamiliar opponents.

  Plan consistency: percentage of planned prerequisites progressed,
  invalidation detection, quality of fallback decisions, interventions that
  make the chosen move change when dependencies are perturbed.

  Independence from teacher: pure student, full teacher, mixed policy,
  distilled student, and teacher-removed tests. A mentor's strength does
  not imply the student's strength.

  Distinctiveness: corpus-rarity of motifs controlling for opponent strength,
  opening and position type, plus novelty outside whole datasets. Unseen in a
  corpus != never seen in chess history.

  Device performance: cold-start time, warm P50/P95/P99 neural pass,
  end-to-end move time, total memory/RSS, sustained game thermal/energy
  draw, battery and time-control violations.

F.5. ADVERSARIAL FAILURE CASES

  - Unusual move is actually a forced blunder against correct defense.
  - Beautiful plan only works in one forced/cooperative generated line.
  - 'Hidden concept' is correlated with famous master's opening rather
    than a reusable strategic mechanism.
  - Persistent plan state keeps steering a refuted idea.
  - A model trained on deep engine targets learns conventional engine moves,
    thereby erasing the artistic candidate policy.
  - PRM rewards being consistent even when switching plans is necessary.
  - Offline novelty objective overfits the evaluator's shallow blind spots.
  - Looped model becomes overconfident without improvement.
  - Higher parameter count increases latency without improving strength.
  - Model quality collapses after INT4 quantization, AOT compilation or
    mobile NPU operator substitution.
  - Expert judges reward visually spectacular sacrifices but ignore their
    actual objective cost.
  - New architecture loses to a simple small search + pretrained policy.

F.6. THE SIX-HEAD QUARANTINE RULE

Do not enable a sixth/emergent expert merely because a training run produced
some novel-looking moves. Pre-register a separate objective and show its
incremental benefit against five-head controls. Preserve a switch to disable
it and a model version/hash. Novelty is a claim to verify, not branding.

APPENDIX G. ON-DEVICE ENGINEERING: ONEPLUS 15, 16 GB RAM, 8 ELITE GEN 5

G.1. HARD FACTS AND RESEARCH STATUS

Owner-specified physical device: OnePlus 15, 16 GB RAM, Snapdragon 8 Elite
Gen 5. The exact device and RAM are a requirement sourced from the user;
this file does not claim to have inspected the handset. All latency numbers
in the discussion are TARGETS or examples, not actual OnePlus measurements.
A smartphone's usable model memory is below total physical RAM because
Android, the app, GPU/NPU execution buffers, caches and other processes also
consume memory. Thermal constraints become important over long games.

G.2. PARAMETER STORAGE ROUGH MATH

Raw weights only, before activation, optimizer, quantization metadata or
platform buffers:

    79 million parameters at INT8  ≈ 79 MB decimal.
    120 million parameters at INT8 ≈ 120 MB decimal.
    350 million parameters at INT8 ≈ 350 MB decimal.
    1 billion parameters at INT8   ≈ 1 GB decimal.
    3 billion parameters at 4-bit  ≈ 1.5 GB decimal.
    7 billion parameters at 4-bit  ≈ 3.5 GB decimal.

These estimates do not imply that the model, attention operations, batch size,
NPU operator coverage or runtime will deliver low move latency. A larger model
may underperform a smaller chess specialist in both strength and speed.

G.3. SIZES CONSIDERED OVER THE CONVERSATION

    Early 5–20M baseline: fast but may lack competitive capacity.
    Maia3-79M backbone: known research donor, architecture compatibility
      and weights/license to verify before adoption.
    50–150M / 120M student: plausible prototype exploration.
    150–500M / 120–350M: ambitious but potentially manageable options.
    1–3B total sparse MoE: possible only if actual active computation,
      memory traffic and mobile operator support justify complexity.
    4B+ language-model teacher: offline training/annotation candidate,
      not a default on-device move-selection component.

Do not choose size by rhetoric. Use a benchmark sweep measuring both Elo and
verified creative quality against real p50/p95 latency.

G.4. RUNTIME IMPLEMENTATION PATH OPTIONS

Option A: Native Android/Kotlin minSdk 35 host + local rules engine +
          on-device neural inference + embedded/native UCI critic.
Option B: Native C++/Rust chess core + JNI API to Kotlin interface, with
          model execution via supported Android runtime.
Option C: One UCI-compatible local engine binary invoked by a light app.

Pick one based on the owner's final integration requirement, weights and
supported frameworks. No new expenses, paid APIs, cloud inference or data
collection without owner authorization.

The training ecosystem need not be Kotlin. Python/PyTorch is a reasonable
research environment, followed by model export (e.g. supported ONNX or
other deployment graph), validation of semantic and numeric parity, and
Android compilation. Stockfish and other binaries have independent license
obligations; do not assume free use implies permission to relicense.

G.5. EFFICIENCY PRINCIPLES

  - Load/checkpoint model once, not per move.
  - Compile operators once, with warm and cold benchmarks separately.
  - Legal move generation is deterministic and CPU-cheap relative to huge
    neural evaluation; never rely on a model to decide legality.
  - Reuse recurrent plan-state and exact board history across moves.
  - Use shared backbone with small heads where beneficial.
  - Distill expensive research methods into a direct policy.
  - Use selective objective search on uncertain, materially important
    candidate decisions rather than unconditional enormous search.
  - Measure NPU/GPU/CPU *latency* for batch 1 and alternative batch sizes;
    don't equate throughput with speed of one move.
  - Ensure low memory allocation and predictable peak RAM use.
  - Observe thermals under sustained 30+ minute and multi-game loads.
  - Test battery and offline behavior without internet permissions.
  - Store exact seeds, model/checkpoint hashes, operator backend and app
    version for reproducibility.

G.6. LATENCY TARGETS (NOT VERIFIED)

    Baseline single pass target: ideally <100 ms after warm-up.
    Routine complete move target: ideally <250 ms.
    Critical move initial target: <1 second where feasible, with
       compute/time-control-aware expansion if owner desires.
    P95 and P99 are as important as average latency.
    If 1–6 recurrent loops cost more than comparable tiny search, keep the
       simpler alternative; if under budget, explore both.

No statement of '15 ms for 79M', '40 ms for 32-node search', or 'B8 free on
NPU' is a measured fact in this document.

G.7. OFFLINE PRIVACY AND ENGINE MODES

Keep chess games, model weights, player settings and optional performance
telemetry local by default. No phone-to-cloud inference dependency. The
user can export research logs deliberately. Disclosed bot, private sparring
and analysis modes can be separated from future regulated online play.
Engine strength/profile settings should have honest labels based on measured
calibration, not literal proof of biological thinking or any assertion about
third-party detector behavior.

APPENDIX H. CHESS.COM FAIR-PLAY RESEARCH: HISTORICAL CONTEXT, NOT FINAL ENGINE
================================================================================

During the founder's ideation, Chess.com's public enforcement research was
studied as a comparator for perceived engine-vs-human behavioral differences.
The public system was described as multi-factor statistical analysis across
games, expert review in titled cases, account context and special-event
proctoring. Chess.com does not publish all private features or thresholds.
Neither a particular move-matching accuracy, artificial error schedule nor
new neural architecture can establish that a bot will be accepted as human.

The founder later supplied ideas involving distribution fitting, predicted
move-time profiles, first-account calibration and surrogate classifier
constraints. These are preserved in this handoff at the level of historical
research provenance and mathematical critiques in Appendix D. They are not
components of CHIMERA's offline intelligence roadmap or instructions for
bypassing a platform's fair-play enforcement.

Transferable legitimate questions:
  * Can we scientifically distinguish patterns produced by search-heavy,
    searchless, imitation-trained, plan-based and hybrid chess systems?
  * Can expert players explain the plans and mistakes of these systems?
  * How much performance variance arises from genuine bounded reasoning?
  * Which cognitive variables are robust across skill levels and positions?
  * Does a new architecture produce more coherent long-range plans than
    comparable conventional engines?

Testing should use public approved research data, offline simulations,
consenting participants and properly disclosed bot tournaments.

N.3 MULTI-SACRIFICE GENERATOR (#156 + #164 + #163)

  A. From legal game positions, identify rare multi-piece sacrifice
     sequences and king-drag motifs. Track SAN/UCI and exact material delta.
  B. Generate candidate forcing continuations without assuming the
     defender cooperates. Include captures, interpositions, king escapes,
     and positional counter-defenses.
  C. Use an independent engine at increasing budgets to challenge them.
  D. Label: VERIFIED WITHIN SEARCH BUDGET / REFUTED / UNKNOWN.
     'Sound against all play' is justified only when objectively solved.
  E. Train a student policy on both good examples AND matching failed ones.
  F. Compare new policy to same-size baseline on unseen source positions.

N.4 QUIET DAGGERS, ZUGZWANG, FORTRESSES (#158, #165, #166)

  A quiet move is a legal non-checking move. That by itself does not
  establish a forced mate, a win, or even an advantage.
  Zugzwang: the player to move has only worsening legal choices.
  Checkmate: the king is in check with zero legal moves.
  Stalemate: the player is not in check with zero legal moves.
  These cases MUST NOT be conflated.

  Design supervised tasks: classify legal quiet moves, discover forcing
  continuations, verify zugzwang claims, label fortress vs breakable
  position with strong defense and tablebases where available.
  Include negative examples (beautiful-looking but losing quiet moves).

N.5 OPENING DISRUPTION & DEFENSE UNDER PRESSURE (#157, #159, #162)

  Repertoire can favor surprise, uncommon pawn structures, and attacking
  positions, with objective soundness reported honestly. Opponent strength
  and time control can select a legitimate competitive-risk profile in
  private offline play. Don't assume a 2700+ player is forced out of prep.
  In losing positions, seek legal swindles, repetitions, perpetual checks,
  or defensible tactical counterplay. Don't forbid the best drawing chance.

N.6 EXPLANATIONS AND MEMORY (#133, #152)

  Maintain structured objective, resources, plan preconditions, opponent
  counterplan, and fallback. Test causal faithfulness with controlled
